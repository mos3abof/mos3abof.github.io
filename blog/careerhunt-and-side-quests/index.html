CareerHunt App and Other Stories!

A collection of stories of why and how I built an open source job tracking rust application, and other projects!

Oct 26, 2026

Intro

I am looking for a job 1. If you have ever been in that position, you know the drill; dozens of applications, each one at a different stage, recruiters whose names you half remember, interview dates scribbled somewhere, job descriptions that vanish from the company's careers page the moment you need to re-read them, and a spreadsheet that starts tidy and becomes a crime scene by the second week.

I wanted a simple way to keep track of all of it: applications, interviews, contacts, notes, and progress. I looked around, could not quite find what I wanted, so I did what any reasonable engineer would do, and built a little app and deployed it on my local Kubernetes cluster!

App built == Problem solved, right?

Well, my brain had other plans. 😅

The post's title is an ode to a collection of stories I read in school, and forgot everything about it except the title, and the plethora of feelings and imaginative worlds it immersed me in. That state very aptly describes what you are about to read next! The novel's title was:

Thirteen O'clock and Other Stories 2

by Enid Blyton

What followed was a few weeks of hyperfocus, one rabbit hole after the other (and sometimes in parallel), and a multi-layered scope creep that would make any project manager weep. Let me walk you through it, one side quest at a time.

✦ ✦ ✦

Side Quest #1: A Design System

The little app needed a UI, and I did not want it to look like every other admin dashboard on the internet. Arabesque Labs, my software venture, did not have a visual identity yet either. Two birds, one stone.

A few weeks later, I had the Arabesque design system: the tokens, CSS, components and rules that I build everything at Arabesque Labs with.

A few core things (among others) I cared about from day one:

  • Bidirectional from the start: English and Arabic, left-to-right and right-to-left, as first class citizens. Not as an afterthought bolted on when someone files a bug about a mirrored arrow.
  • Efficient interaction: people should get things done in the fewest steps, with actions that say what they do, and every consequence shown before, during, and after.

And, of course, the geometric patterns. The eight-pointed star tile is the mark, and the patterns you see on the background of every Arabesque page are drawn from it.

It is open source, and you can browse it at design.arabesquelabs.com, or read the source on GitHub. Fair warning though: it is still experimental, and things will move around until it reaches 1.0.

Side Quest #2: A Rebrand

At some point the little app stopped being little. It grew into a proper project, so it deserved a proper name and a proper home: CareerHunt.

A name, a domain, a landing page, a logo that uses the Arabesque star... You know how it goes. 😅

Side Quest #3: Custom Documentation

A proper project needs proper documentation. I love Docusaurus, and I have used it many times before. But now that I have a design system, I could not just use Docusaurus as is, right?!

So I built Arabesque for Docusaurus, a Docusaurus 3 theme on top of Arabesque. It restyles the classic theme with the design system's tokens, typography and components, maps Infima (Docusaurus' CSS framework) onto them, and even brings its own colours for code highlighting. The result: a documentation site that looks and reads like the rest of Arabesque, in light and dark, and in left-to-right and right-to-left alike.

And because no rabbit hole is complete without going one level deeper, I even remixed the Docusaurus dinosaur using geometric patterns. Meet the Arabesquosaurus! (Okay, I just made that name up, but I think it might stick.)

The theme is published on npm, and comes with its own documentation site, built with, you guessed it, the theme itself. Both CareerHunt's documentation and the design system's documentation use it.

Side Quest #4: Power Close Gets a Facelift

Once you have a design system, everything you have ever built starts looking a bit outdated.

Power Close is a browser extension I first built back in 2014, for the tab addicts among us (yes, I am one of them). You type a domain name or a keyword, hit Enter, and every tab with it in the address is closed, across all your windows. Many Facebook tabs open, scattered across windows? Type facebook.com, press Enter, and they are gone.

Twelve years later, it got the Arabesque treatment: a new logo, a redesigned popup, and a brand new website.

And since I was already in there, it also picked up a few features I had been meaning to add for, well, years:

  • The popup counts the matching tabs as you type, so you see what will close before anything does.
  • Suggestions from the domains of your open tabs, as you type.
  • A keyboard shortcut: Alt+Shift+P.
  • A right-click menu option: Close all tabs from this domain.
  • Settings, synced with your browser.
  • Support for Firefox for Android.

You can get it from the Chrome Web Store or Firefox Add-ons.

Side Quest #5: A New Zola Theme!

And finally, the one that hits close to home, literally. This very website got a new theme: Arabesque for Zola. If things look different around here, that is why!

This one was particularly important for me. My blog has been bilingual for a while now, with the old Arabic posts imported back from Blogger. Getting a theme that handles both English and Arabic properly has always been a struggle, and usually involved me overriding half the theme's CSS. This time round, right-to-left is built in: each language gets its own address, the header links each page to its translation, and right-to-left pages mirror, while numbers, code, and the pattern do not.

On top of that:

  • Light and dark: it follows your system, with a button to switch between system, light, and dark. It remembers your choice, with no flash.
  • Templates for everything: a home page, paginated sections, tags, an archive by year, search, and a 404 page.
  • Nineteen shortcodes for Markdown: the design system's components that Markdown cannot write, plus galleries, separators, Mermaid diagrams, and YouTube, PDF, Gist, and Instagram embeds.
  • Nothing from elsewhere: the fonts, CSS, and scripts are all in the theme. No tracking, and no framework.

It is open source as well, and the GitHub repository doubles as the demo site.

But I Digress!

So, back to the main quest!

Somewhere along the way, CareerHunt stopped being just a tool for my own homelab, and became something I wanted to share, in the hope that it might make someone's life a little bit better.

So, What Is CareerHunt?

CareerHunt helps you manage your job search in one place: from applications and interview stages, to the notes and details you would otherwise scatter across spreadsheets, documents, and browser tabs.

The idea is simple: your job search, on your own infrastructure, with your data under your control.

Notable Features

Below is a list of notable features, and you can seel the full list here.

  • Application pipeline: Track applications as they move through stages such as Applied, Interviewing, Offer, and Rejected.
  • Application details: Keep job descriptions, notes, contact information, salary details, and everything else about an application in one place.
  • Self-hosted and privacy-first: Your job search data lives on infrastructure you control, not on a third-party hosted service.
  • A custom UI: A clean interface built with the open-source Arabesque design system.
  • Flexible deployment: Run it with Docker Compose, or deploy it to Kubernetes.

And a lot of little things that I hope you will like.

Self-hosting

CareerHunt is designed with self-hosting in mind. Both Docker Compose and Kubernetes deployment options are documented in the quick start guide. I would especially love feedback from people who run their own services: what is missing, what could be simpler, and what would make this something you would actually use?

These are the Early Days

This is an early release, and there is still plenty I would like to improve. But I figured it was better to get it out there, learn from real users, and improve it in the open, than to keep polishing it forever. (Also, if I kept polishing it, I would probably end up building a custom font next. Do not tempt me.)

If you are job hunting, maintaining a homelab, or just enjoy trying out open-source projects, give CareerHunt a spin. Bug reports, feature requests, contributions, constructive criticism, jokes, memes, rants, or any type of feedback really, are all welcome. ❤️

I truly hope CareerHunt makes someone's job hunt a little less chaotic.
